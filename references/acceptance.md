# Completion checks for the adapted OS foundation

Match verification to the requested scope. A skill revision needs document validation; an OS fork needs the relevant app checks below. Completing a new real journey is not an acceptance requirement here.

## Source reuse

- Launch inputs record the vertical, industry scope/assumptions, target environment and workspace, app identity, source choice and delivery intent. Hosted destination identities come from supported tools; source and target are kept distinct.
- The app derives from actual Banking/Retail source, with a recorded base OS and revision. The unchanged copy builds before adaptation.
- Banking source is the original Trusted Workforce Console, using an authorized hosted app or checkout supplied for this installation. The local checkout's relationship to that app is verified or any unresolved revision difference is documented; the Atlas derivative is not mistaken for the authoritative Banking source.
- The existing shell, sphere assets/components, alignment/geometry, shared renderers, interactions, styles, zoom and navigation are reused. A lookalike rebuild is not a completed clone.
- The reuse/adaptation map identifies retained flows and meaningful changes. New components/flows have a concrete reason.
- Unchanged useful journey topologies, branching, node alignment and connector routing carry over. No artificial redesign is required just to differentiate the industry.
- Source apps and unrelated tenant resources remain unchanged. Identity, namespace, visible customer content and links belong to the new vertical.
- A fresh chat can locate the complete skill resources and actual source app. The skill folder alone is not represented as a bundled copy of Banking/Retail OS.

## Research and architecture

- Department choices and material journey changes trace to current evidence or labeled assumptions/proposals.
- The research workbook identifies the industry segment, region, business model, value chain and important differences between a generic industry model and a named customer's verified operating model.
- The evidence coverage matrix covers core operations, customer/partner channels, corporate functions, systems/records, economics, decisions and exceptions. Critical gaps remain visibly provisional.
- Journey research describes current work stage by stage with accountable roles, inputs/outputs, systems, cross-team handoffs, exception paths and outcome/KPI definitions. Proposed automation is distinguished from that current-state evidence.
- Material claims are read in their underlying sources, scoped and dated, and cross-checked where possible. Applicable consequential rules have primary-source support or an explicit unresolved gap; source conflicts are not silently merged.
- Reused flows pass the industry-fit review for actors, stage meaning, records, sequence, decision authority and exceptions. Shared topology is acceptable; industry terms are supported by industry substance.
- Finance and Legal were considered alongside core operations and relevant shared services. Agent/team sizes reflect business importance, distinct workflow breadth and specialist needs, with evidence or labeled design assumptions.
- The workforce allocation table classifies departments as core, enabling or corporate support and identifies primary core departments before counts are chosen. Reject a repeated quota such as four agents per department, near-even core/support coverage, symmetry padding and duplicate specialists.
- For a full industry OS, compute distribution checks from unique canonical workers: core business has more than half of all agents; the smallest primary core department has at least twice the agents of the largest individual non-core department by default; and HR has strictly fewer agents than every core business department. Counts, shares, rationale and actual check results are recorded. Skills, human gates and repeated journey appearances do not inflate counts. Revisit real core coverage and consolidate overlapping support roles if checks fail; do not fabricate roles or classifications to pass.
- Business meaning governs classification: in telco, core network operations, provisioning and service assurance are not mistaken for generic internal IT. Primary core functions receive visibly greater coverage than HR and other support functions. Agents, Org, filters and inherited proportional ring sectors agree with the allocation; sectors are not equalized. An explicit narrower user scope is documented when applicable.
- IDs, ownership, memberships, filters, counts, passports and journey participation agree across the existing views.
- Skills retain a parent worker and do not inflate workforce counts. Skill and Human gate labels appear on cards and popups.
- New journeys have useful clickable architectures and are Planned. Any retained Active capability has been checked for the new context; source working-journey counts are not blindly copied.
- Useful inherited execution infrastructure can remain without creating new cases or surfacing irrelevant customer data.

## Visual and interaction fidelity

- Source/fork comparisons at matching viewport and zoom confirm sphere sizes, relative spacing, alignment, labels, highlighting and motion remain faithful. Data-driven changes in membership use the original geometry.
- Every intended department, team and agent name is complete and readable. Check first/last letters, SVG glyph extents, guide length, neighboring labels and container bounds after fonts load at desktop, shared-screen and mobile widths. Reject clipped fragments such as `OURCIN` for `SOURCING`; correct DOM text or a tooltip alone does not pass.
- Every worker sphere is bright, colorful and fully opaque in Agents, Journeys, Org and details. Inspect the asset itself plus computed filters/opacity on the image and its ancestors. Planned/proposed roles, unimplemented agents, selected/unselected workers, filters and scoped viewers do not introduce grayscale or dimming; gray/silver assignments are replaced, and canonical worker colors agree across views.
- An explicit Light/Dark control is in the top-right app header on every page, with keyboard access, visible focus and an exposed selected state. Both options remain reachable at desktop, shared-screen and mobile widths without horizontal page overflow. Light is the default; both choices persist across navigation and reloads through an app-specific key, and the control works when storage is unavailable.
- Both themes cover the shell, canvas, cards, tabs, toggle indicators, text, labels, icons, menus, inputs, popups, passports and other inherited pages, including Agents/Journeys/Org. Inspect semantic-token aliases used by shared components to catch unintended white cards or unreadable text in Dark. Appearance changes remain scoped to the app, preserve selection, complete names and colorful spheres, and do not start connection motion. Check the calm-connection requirements below in both modes.
- The Agents page shows zero connection lines or dots at rest and after choosing only a department/journey filter. Hovering or clicking/keyboard-selecting an agent reveals only that agent's existing handoffs. Pointer exit clears hover motion; a clicked selection remains pinned until cleared, then restores the calm view. Verify slow motion using timed frames or a short recording (roughly one quarter of the original speed, at least four seconds per traversal), preserve path geometry, and keep every worker colorful. No invented links, traffic or execution claims are introduced. Reduced-motion mode keeps the focused agent's connections static, and hidden views stop unnecessary motion.
- Department/team/agent selection, relationship highlights, filters, passports, Org drill-through and journey navigation behave as in the source.
- Far-apart catalog selections stack together, including reused/adapted/imported previews. Removing a middle journey closes the gap and fit frames visible content.
- Detail controls remain compact, collapsible, dockable/pop-out where appropriate, keyboard usable and stable while the list scrolls.
- Motion changes refine existing shared components. Reduced-motion mode and pause/reduce controls retain understandable architecture and selection.
- Desktop, shared-screen width and mobile preserve readable local diagram scrolling and reachable controls. Hidden views avoid unnecessary animation work.

## Build and handover

- Run relevant type/build/package checks from the current app instructions. Preserve SDK and source-in-bundle contracts.
- Deliver the app when requested, research brief, reuse map, adapted journey catalog, actual check results and material limitations.
- Note future per-journey demo preparation at a high level without requiring or starting it here. No new realistic cases, conversations, replay/decision engines or audit histories are needed to mark the OS foundation complete.
- Publish only within existing task authorization and destination; do not initiate messages, review requests or new approval workflows.

## Proposed first-use review prompts

These are suggested reviews, not completed evaluations:

1. “Clone Retail OS into Healthcare OS for a US outpatient network. Keep the spheres, their alignment, navigation and shared flows. Research departments and journeys, including Finance and Legal; leave real demo journeys for separate tasks.” Review actual code reuse, the evidence workbook and value chain, stage-level journey research, known vs proposed systems, source-flow fit, faithful geometry and scope boundaries.
2. “Use Banking OS as the base for Insurance OS. Keep suitable document collection and approval flow structures, changing the labels, roles and systems where necessary.” Review source-flow mapping, meaningful adaptation and avoidance of unnecessary graph/layout rewrites.
3. “Make the cloned OS feel more futuristic while keeping exactly how the spheres are arranged and selected.” Review incremental shared motion improvements, unchanged alignment and interactions, and no added case-execution work.
4. “Adapt the OS to departments with short sectors and long names, including Sourcing and Risk & Compliance.” Review complete visible labels, first/last glyphs, guide lengths, bounds and overlap across desktop/mobile without changing sphere or colored-arc geometry.
5. “All agents are proposed, and I am viewing one department. Keep them colorful, but show slow connection dots only when I hover or click an agent.” Review full-color assets and opacity across Agents/Journeys/Org and details, consistent identities, no idle/filter-only lines or dots, focused-agent handoffs, slow motion, hover exit and selection clearing, truthful Planned status, unchanged scope access, reduced motion and hidden views.
6. “Add a Light/Dark option in the top right and remember my choice. Keep the agent map calm in either mode.” Review header placement and mobile reachability, keyboard and selected state, both choices after reload/navigation, app-specific persistence and storage fallback, app-scoped styles, coverage across views and details, full-color workers and complete names, preserved selection, and no idle/filter-only connection lines or dots in either theme.
7. “Create a full Telco OS for a mobile network operator. Size the workforce around the business rather than giving every department four agents.” Review core/enabling/support classifications; network, provisioning and assurance coverage; work-based specialist roles; core-majority and primary-core-to-support comparisons; HR smaller than every core department; compact support skills; no invented staffing or decorative workers; and matching canonical counts and ring proportions. An allocation of four agents each to Network Operations, Service Delivery and HR fails this review.

For a skill-only revision, validate the documents and resource links; do not create or republish an OS merely to validate the wording. The Insurance OS clipped-name, gray-agent, connection-motion and Light/Dark corrections are regression examples for these requirements. Record only checks actually run; these review prompts are not completed benchmark runs.
